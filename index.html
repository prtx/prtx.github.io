Research$ ls ~/research

My research is machine learning for electronic design automation (EDA), which means I:

  • build models that predict timing, parasitics, and wirelength early in the design flow
  • work with representation learning: turning netlists and layouts into representations that one model can reuse across many prediction tasks, and that hold up on circuits and process nodes it has never seen

And because good models need good data, I build open datasets and benchmarks so anyone can train, test, and compare them.

Drexel ICE Lab

EDA-Learn

Most of what you need to do machine learning for chip design is locked away: proprietary designs, commercial tools, and results nobody outside can reproduce. That makes it hard for students and small labs to join in, and hard for anyone to tell which ideas actually work.

EDA-Learn aims to open it all up. EDA-Schema is the shared data: 7,776 physical design runs across 18 circuits on four open process kits, packaged as twelve ready-to-use benchmark tasks. EDA-OpenML (in progress) turns a single config file into a trained, evaluated model. A public leaderboard scores every model against the design tool's own estimate, so claims can be checked rather than taken on trust.

The data, code, and results are all open, so the field can build on shared ground instead of each group rebuilding its own. EDA-Schema won Best Paper at GLSVLSI 2024.
